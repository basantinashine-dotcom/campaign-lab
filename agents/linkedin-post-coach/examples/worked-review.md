# Worked example: a product-learning post

The input in [product-learning.json](product-learning.json) is fictional. This is an illustrative review showing the intended editorial standard; the prompt-builder CLI does not generate reviews.

## Overall assessment

**Needs revision.** The story gives a clear example of a product decision and the feedback behind it. Its main weakness is treating one person's reaction as proof of a universal preference.

## What works

- **“I thought the hard part would be the streak counter”** gives the reader a concrete expectation that the story then challenges.
- **“I changed the screen to show completed days alongside the streak.”** explains the actual product response rather than ending with a generic lesson.

## Prioritized improvements

### 1. High · Specificity and evidence

- **Excerpt:** “This proves everyone prefers encouragement over accountability.”
- **Why it matters:** One friend's response does not establish what everyone prefers. The draft also does not demonstrate that encouragement and accountability are mutually exclusive.
- **Suggested change:** “That feedback made me reconsider how the app responds to a missed day.”
- **Why this helps:** It keeps the lesson grounded in the experience the author supplied and avoids a claim the story cannot support.

### 2. Low · Closing

- **Excerpt:** “What do you think?”
- **Why it matters:** The question gives readers little guidance about which part of the story to respond to.
- **Suggested change:** “How have you designed the moment when a user misses a goal?”
- **Why this helps:** It invites relevant examples from the intended audience of product managers.

## Claims to check

The universal preference claim would require broader evidence than this story provides. The revised sentence above limits the claim to the author's own response. The story itself has not been independently verified.

## One next step

Replace the universal claim with the narrower lesson. The rest of the story can remain close to the original.
