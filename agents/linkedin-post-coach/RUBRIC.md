# Review rubric

This rubric defines editorial judgments, not LinkedIn ranking rules.

| Dimension | Question the coach asks | Useful feedback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reader relevance | Who is this for, and why might they care? | Identify unexplained context or a mismatch with the stated audience. |
| Opening | Does the opening communicate the topic and a reason to read? | Replace vague setup with a concrete point already supported by the draft. |
| Clarity and structure | Can a reader follow one main idea? | Explain what a vague term means or where the argument changes direction. |
| Specificity and evidence | Are claims supported and examples concrete? | Ask for evidence or a real example; distinguish observation from inference. |
| Voice | Does the writing sound consistent with the author's supplied preferences? | Preserve distinctive language; remove repetition or accidental template artifacts. |
| Closing | Does the ending serve the author's purpose? | Suggest a specific invitation or takeaway only where it adds value. |

## Priority

- **High:** A misleading claim, missing essential context, or unclear central message prevents an adequate review or risks a material misunderstanding.
- **Medium:** The point is understandable, but an edit would make its value or reasoning clearer.
- **Low:** An optional wording or formatting improvement.

Do not equate a preferred hook style or punctuation choice with a high-priority problem.

## What makes feedback trustworthy?

Every issue should have a grounded excerpt or a clearly identified missing element, an explanation, and an actionable next step. A proposed rewrite must not add facts. A strong post may need no substantial changes. Actual distribution and reader response cannot be predicted from this rubric.
